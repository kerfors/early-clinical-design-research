Early clinical design research

How the design content of a clinical trial programme can be held as linked knowledge, and what the documents carry today. Two public master protocols are the cases: the Lilly Chronic Pain Master Protocol (CPMP) and REMAP-CAP. Programme-level design and Target Product Profiles (TPP) are the wider frame, after the EFPIA Smart Program Design and PhUSE CDD Framework work (2013–2018).

Status: early exploratory work. Draft, not normative. It is published so the method and the evidence can be followed and reused, not as a model to adopt. Built from public documents (ClinicalTrials.gov, remapcap.org, EU-PEARL, FDA, WOAH, NIH SEED) and public ontologies (OBI, MONDO, HP). Analyst readings are marked as such, and each carries its review state; what still waits for a trial-design reviewer or a clinical reader is listed in the catalogue.

Relation to usdm-rdf and m11-rdf. usdm-rdf gives USDM v4 an RDF/OWL form, and m11-rdf gives every ICH M11 section and element its own identifier. Both describe one study and one protocol. This repo explores what neither covers yet: design content that is shared and handed over between the documents of a master protocol, across sub-studies and versions. USDM v4 stays the document carrier here (usdm-master-protocol/, checked with the usdm-rdf validator), and the design modules sit next to it. Pointing statements at m11-rdf section identifiers, rather than at section numbers, is an open choice.

The two cases

  • CPMP (H0P-MC-CPMP): 4 compounds × 3 disease states. One master protocol, 3 disease-state addenda (DSA, not public) and 12 intervention-specific appendices (ISA), each posted on ClinicalTrials.gov under its own NCT ID. The 13 Schedules of Activities are extracted to USDM in soa2usdm-collections.
  • REMAP-CAP: an adaptive platform trial in community-acquired pneumonia. A Core Protocol (versions 4.0 and 4.1), the Patient, Pathogen and Disease Appendix (PANDA) and nine domain-specific appendices (DSA).

Templates as data

Before the cases, two public template families were read as data, the same method on a template rather than a protocol: EU-PEARL (sections with keys, text categories from the template's own markers, field labels, a TOC check, and the MPT ↔ ISA alignment) and four TPP templates (column models compared, with concept matching left to a review file).
