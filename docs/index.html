Early clinical design research

How the design content of a clinical trial programme can be identified, and its relations across documents made explicit, as data; and what the documents carry today. Two public master protocols are the cases: the Lilly Chronic Pain Master Protocol (CPMP) and REMAP-CAP. Programme-level design and Target Product Profiles (TPP) are the wider frame, after the EFPIA Smart Program Design and PhUSE CDD Framework work (2013–2018).

Status: early exploratory work. Draft, not normative. It is published so the method and the evidence can be followed and reused, not as a model to adopt. Built from public documents (ClinicalTrials.gov, remapcap.org, EU-PEARL, FDA, WOAH, NIH SEED). The design classes are anchored in 16 terms from public ontologies (OBI with IAO and BFO, MONDO, HP), imported from pinned releases; the gaps found are listed in the module READMEs. Analyst readings are marked as such, and each carries its review state; what still waits for a trial-design reviewer or a clinical reader is listed in the catalogue.

Relation to usdm-rdf and m11-rdf. usdm-rdf gives USDM v4 an RDF/OWL form, and m11-rdf gives every ICH M11 section and element its own identifier. Both describe one study and one protocol. This repo explores what neither covers yet: design content that is shared and handed over between the documents of a master protocol, across sub-studies and versions. The first round built both master protocols as USDM v4 JSON, validated with the usdm-rdf validator, and collected where USDM v4.0 could not carry them as a table of breakpoints (usdm-master-protocol/). The design modules sit next to USDM for what it cannot carry. Pointing statements at m11-rdf section identifiers, rather than at section numbers, is an open choice.

Identifiers, for now. The design modules are OWL files whose IRIs sit in a namespace for now (https://kerfors.github.io/early-clinical-design-research/), and those IRIs do not resolve yet.

Where this is heading: first the design content as Linked Data, as in m11-rdf, then the ontologies behind the content.
