Design modules

Early exploratory work. The design modules are the second round of this work, after USDM. The design content of each case is read from its documents and held as OWL classes, so that a reasoner can tell designs apart and find what they share.

  • A component is one unit of design content: an eligibility criterion, an objective, an endpoint, a Schedule of Activities cell (CPMP), a statement (REMAP-CAP). It has one identity wherever it occurs; where the wording differs, that identity is a decision (Q1).
  • A module groups the components of one layer or one shared set. CPMP: the master protocol, three disease states (not public, so only what is handed over to them), four compounds and twelve ISAs; each ISA module imports the master, its disease state and its compound. REMAP-CAP: the Core Protocol (one module per version, 4.0 and 4.1, under a version-independent Core), PANDA and nine DSAs.
  • The reasoner then shows what no document states: in CPMP, a compound module of 26 components shared by the three ISAs of each compound (CP-1); in REMAP-CAP, that no DSA sits under either Core version, because no DSA names one (Q7).

What this is not: a design ontology. The modules reuse 16 upstream terms and pass an OWL 2 DL check, but no ontologist has reviewed them, and they were built only for the competency questions of two cases. A foundation that scales takes more. For every kind of content it has to be decided what a term denotes, the act performed, the information it produces or the plan for either, and that choice has to be aligned with BFO and the ontologies reused. The modules here are test cases for what such a model must represent (Findings, Vision).
